# Intake 03 — Projects

**Copy the block below once per project.** Four to six projects is a good launch.

The description is what turns a photo album into a portfolio. It is the single
biggest difference between a site that wins work and one that just looks nice.

Hand this back and I'll create the folders and write the `project.json` files.

---

## How many, and which

Pick your **strongest** work, not your most recent. A visitor will look at three
projects and form an opinion. Make sure the first three are the ones you'd want
them to see.

Order them deliberately — project `01` appears first in the grid.

---

## Photo checklist per project

| Slot | Count | Final size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cover | 1 | 1200×900 (4:3) | The grid thumbnail. Must read at small size — usually a clear, simple, wide shot |
| Hero | 1 | 2400×1350 (16:9) | The banner at the top of the project page. Often your single best image |
| Gallery | 6–12 | 2000×1333 (3:2) | The sequence. Order matters |

**Cover and hero are usually different images.** The cover competes with eleven
others in a grid, so it needs to be legible at 400px wide. The hero has the whole
screen, so it can be atmospheric.

**Sequencing the gallery.** The best project pages read like a walk through the
building:

1. Arrival / approach
2. Exterior, the whole building
3. The principal space
4. Secondary spaces
5. A detail — a material, a junction, a piece of joinery
6. Something quiet to end on — evening light, a view out

Name the files so they sort in that order: `01-approach.jpg`, `02-exterior.jpg`,
`03-living.jpg`, and so on.

**Give me the originals.** Full size, uncompressed, straight from the
photographer. Don't resize or crop anything first.

## Description

Two to four paragraphs minimum. Leave a blank line between paragraphs.

What to cover:
- **The brief and the site.** What was asked for, what was there already, what
  the constraints were.
- **The response.** The key move — the idea that organises the whole building.
  This is the paragraph that matters most.
- **Materials and making.** What it's built of, and why. Local stone, a
  particular craftsman, a technique you worked out on site.
- **The experience.** How it is to be in. Light, air, sequence, views.

Write about the building, not about yourselves. "The verandah roofs are built
with locally sourced Mangalore clay tiles" tells a reader far more than "we
carefully considered material selection".
